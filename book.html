About the book

Women did not simply join parties. They built them.

After decades on the sidelines, women are now central to India’s political and development agenda. Representation from Below traces this transformation away from the halls of power and toward women’s inclusion in local politics—and the mobilizing capacity they built inside political parties.

Drawing on more than a decade of fieldwork, original surveys, and natural experiments across eight elections, Tanushree Goyal shows how women in local politics created grassroots party networks, recruited other women, and expanded parties’ capacity to reach women voters. Once women became electorally consequential, party elites adapted: reshaping policies, investing in women’s organizations, and opening pathways to higher office.

The book reframes descriptive representation as a source of organizational power. Even within clientelist parties and under patriarchal constraints, women can transform the boundaries that restrict them into political leverage—and reshape whom parties mobilize and ultimately serve.

Women are autonomous agents of their own empowerment and party democracy works best when change starts at the bottom. Goyal’s account is both provocative as well as persuasive. It marks her arrival as a major voice in the study of Indian politics.”
